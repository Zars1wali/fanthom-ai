## 3. What to capture

Write captures to `.agent-logs/` in the repo root. These are committed and ship with
your repo, publicly, so treat them as part of the submission.

**We want the prompt and the final response. Nothing in between.**

Not the thinking. Not the tool calls. Not the intermediate steps, the file reads, the
diffs, or the retries the model made on its own. Just: what you asked, and what came
back at the end of that turn.

Capture, per turn:

- The prompt, verbatim and in full. No truncation, no paraphrase, no cleanup.
- The final response for that prompt, in full, including the ones where it got it wrong.
- A UTC timestamp.
- The model name, so a switch mid-build is visible.

Do not:

- Add `.agent-logs/` to `.gitignore`. It ships with the repo.
- Edit, tidy, summarise, or delete an entry after the fact. A messy honest log scores
  better than a clean one, and we can tell the difference.

Commit the logs as you go, interleaved with the code they produced, not in one dump at
the end. The commit order shows the order you actually worked in.

## 2. Three Decisions I Am Proudest Of

1. **The Typed Content Layer Architecture (`src/content/`)**:
   Instead of scattering unmaintainable markup across 90 different JSX files, 17 parameterized templates render purely from typed content schemas. Adding a new integration, comparison, API endpoint, or changelog release requires only a single typed JSON/TS object, guaranteeing zero layout shift and total design consistency across all pages.
2. **Unified Single-Origin Experience (`SiteShell`)**:
   Original Fathom splits users across 6+ distinct domains (`fathom.ai`, `fathom.video`, `developers.fathom.ai`, `help.fathom.video`, `trust.fathom.video`, `status.fathom.video`), each with divergent layouts, navigation bars, and styling. We consolidated every destination under a single cohesive origin with universal ⌘K search and consistent wayfinding.
3. **Receipts & Shown-Not-Claimed Everywhere**:
   Rather than treating receipts as an app-only gimmick, the receipts principle permeates the marketing and documentation site. Hero sections, role solutions, and deal views showcase interactive components that seek directly to spoken seconds upon click. All sample data (prices, customer stories, uptime metrics) is honestly badged with `"Sample"`.

## 3. Three Weakest Spots

1. **Third-Party Monogram Visuals**:
   Due to the strict rule against embedding unverified third-party trademarks or raster logos, integrations use high-contrast monogram tiles (e.g., `SF`, `HS`, `SL`). While clean, compliant, and accessible, visitors accustomed to branded SVG logos may find them more austere.
2. **Simulated Background Processing**:
   While the `/live` meeting simulator transitions realistically through transcribing, segmenting, and summarizing, live multi-party audio recording uses deterministic clock playback rather than browser WebRTC capture or native Zoom bot SDKs.
3. **Single Production JavaScript Bundle**:
   The production build bundles the client code into a single chunk (`816 kB` minified / `217 kB` gzipped). While initial load time is well under 1 second on local dev, route-based dynamic code-splitting via `React.lazy()` would further optimize first-paint performance on constrained mobile 3G networks.
